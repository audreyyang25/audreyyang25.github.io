## Abstract

Approximately one in three American adults possesses some form of criminal record. For these individuals, criminal-record clearing, or expungement, holds the potential to facilitate a fresh start. Currently, over two-thirds of U.S. states offer some form of adult criminal-record expungement. However, a persistent gap remains between those who are eligible for expungement and those who ultimately pursue it, an issue commonly referred to as the “expungement gap.” Despite policy interest in record-clearing mechanisms, the causal effects of expungement remain underexplored in the empirical literature.

This study implements a randomized controlled trial (RCT) to estimate the effects of expungement on key life outcomes, including recidivism, housing stability, and employment. In collaboration with Kansas Legal Services, a nonprofit legal aid provider, we randomly assigned expungement-eligible individuals into two groups: one receiving intensive legal assistance through assignment to a specialized expungement attorney (substantially increasing the likelihood of record clearance), and the other receiving only self-help materials, which make expungement less likely. The study is ongoing, and all participants are being followed over a five-year period to assess impacts on criminal justice involvement, employment stability, and housing outcomes.

In addition to quantitative outcome measures acquired from government records such as tax information and wage rates, we collect self-reported survey data by asking for self-perception ratings on a scale from 0 to 10 to explore how expungement affects self-perception and identity. While preliminary quantitative findings from survey responses have not shown significant expungement effects on employment and housing, we hope to find more substantive results from the ongoing data collection.

This study is the first randomized controlled trial to examine the effects of criminal-record expungement on housing stability and recidivism. It also evaluates different interventions that may help close the expungement gap and inform broader policy discussions surrounding criminal justice reform and economic reintegration.
