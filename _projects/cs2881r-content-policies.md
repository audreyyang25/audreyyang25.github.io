_[CS 2881r AI Safety] [Week 5] Content Policies_ is a blog post on LessWrong, co-written with MB Crosier Samuel and published in October 2025. It recaps the fifth week of Harvard's CS 2881r: AI Safety and Alignment, taught by Boaz Barak, which covered content policies, moderation, platform governance, and why enforcement is hard.

<a href="https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/uahJ7CrB8oWyRyyvL/cs-2881r-ai-safety-week-5-content-policies" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read the full post on LessWrong ↗</a>

## What it covers

- **Pre-readings.** Three pieces on how platform moderation tends to evolve, on the working conditions of Facebook's contract moderators, and on what Google Gemini's image-generation controversy showed about the limits of AI.
- **Guest talk.** Ziad Reslan of OpenAI's Product Policy team on where content moderation came from, the tradeoffs it involves, and how generative AI is moderated in layers: written policy, automated screening, and human review. The class also tried drafting image-generation content policies and found the lines surprisingly hard to draw.
- **In-class experiment.** Hugh Van Deventer compared DeepSeek-R1-Qwen3-8B with its safety-trained RealSafe variant on OR-Bench. System prompts helped somewhat, but safety training mattered far more.
