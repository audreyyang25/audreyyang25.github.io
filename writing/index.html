  1. CS 2881r
    Evaluating Orthogonal Projections in Vector Embedding Spaces for Misinformation Detection and Safety-based Semantic Classification
    Eric Gong and Audrey Yang
    Course project, Harvard CS 2881r: AI Safety and Alignment , Dec 2025
    Abs PDF

    Traditional methods for the identification and classification of any semantic content, such as misinformation, rely on computationally taxing means, be it fine-tuning large language models on vast text collections or training neural network models on swathes of labeled vector embeddings. In addition to computational and cost expenditure, these methods become impossible when large amounts of carefully curated and labeled examples are unavailable. Here, we propose and evaluate a novel method for misinformation detection and safety-oriented semantic classification. The method makes use of orthogonal projections of vector embeddings, making the process highly computationally efficient. Most importantly, it is possible to execute without a large corpus of labeled training data.

  1. Metaethics
    Why Should We Care? A Defense of Procedural Realism
    Audrey Yang
    Final essay, Harvard PHIL 173E: Metaethics , May 2026
    Abs PDF

    I reconstruct and defend Christine Korsgaard’s procedural realism. I argue that procedural realism can survive David Enoch’s Schmagency objection and can concede the existence of schmagents while retaining its universality and categoricity.

  2. Metaethics
    In Defense of Street’s Antirealism
    Audrey Yang
    Midterm paper, Harvard PHIL 173E: Metaethics , Mar 2026
    Abs PDF

    In "A Darwinian Dilemma for Realist Theories of Value," Sharon Street challenges moral realists by positing that Darwinian theories of evolution present an inescapable dilemma that makes realism incompatible with natural science. I summarize Street’s argument, discuss two objections from Katia Vavova’s "Evolutionary Debunking of Moral Realism," and propose a way for Street to successfully defend her view against these objections.

  1. Lit. Phil.
    The Cost of True Happiness: An Analysis of Diotima Through the Lens of Austen
    Audrey Yang
    Essay, written for fun , Mar 2025
    Abs PDF

    In Plato’s Symposium, Socrates presents Diotima’s ladder of love, in which progressing up the stages leads the lover to an understanding of virtue and the attainment of happiness. I claim that Jane Austen endorses Diotima’s ladder as a path towards virtue and true happiness, but introduces two additions Diotima does not explicitly address: selflessness is a key trait needed to progress up the ladder, and cultivating selflessness requires the potentially painful sacrifice of fleeting, emotional happiness in exchange for true and lasting happiness.

  2. Lit. Phil.
    Sympathy: the Key to Character Discernment
    Audrey Yang
    Essay, written for fun , May 2025
    Abs PDF

    Jane Austen uses the interaction of characters in her novels to illustrate a method for discerning moral character, and a key part of this process is exercising sympathy, which Adam Smith claims in his Theory of Moral Sentiments allows us to understand the motives behind people’s actions. I propose that Austen presents a cyclic model of sympathy and character judgment, where the two inform one another to allow the sympathizer to converge upon an epistemically sound judgment of character, and that her novels serve as a means for readers to practice this model of character discernment.
